**The quantum simulation and video game job markets** are quite different in 2026: quantum computing/simulation is a high-growth, talent-shortage field with strong demand and premium pay, while video games is a large, mature entertainment industry experiencing stabilization after waves of layoffs, with selective and competitive hiring.

### Quantum Simulation / Quantum Computing Job Market
Quantum simulation (modeling complex quantum systems for chemistry, materials, physics, etc.) is a core application area within the broader quantum computing sector. The market is **booming** due to heavy investment, government support, and advancing hardware.

- **Workforce and Growth**: The pure-play quantum workforce reached ~16,500 in 2025 (up ~2,000 in one year). Projections show ~250,000 new quantum jobs by 2030 and up to 840,000 by 2035. Demand significantly outpaces supply (e.g., estimates of one qualified professional per three open positions).
- **Demand Drivers**: Roles in quantum software, hardware, simulation algorithms, error correction, and applications (chemistry, optimization, ML). Companies, national labs, and tech giants (e.g., IBM, Google, Quantinuum) are hiring. Skills in quantum information science, programming (Qiskit, Cirq, etc.), physics, and hybrid quantum-classical methods are prized.
- **Salaries**: High and rising. U.S. median around $166,000; entry-level often $80k–$140k; senior/research roles $150k–$250k+. Salaries grow faster than traditional tech (e.g., ~10–12% annually in recent years) due to the talent shortage.
- **Outlook**: Very positive for those with relevant skills (PhDs common for research roles, but software/engineering positions are more accessible with targeted training). The field is expanding from research to commercial applications. Challenges include a persistent skills gap and the need for interdisciplinary talent.

**Entry Tips**: Advanced degrees help, but bootcamps, open-source contributions, and hybrid roles (e.g., quantum-literate software engineers) are growing. Focus on simulation use cases for chemistry/materials if that's your interest.

### Video Game Industry Job Market
The games sector is massive (global revenues ~$180–200B+ recently, projected higher) but has faced turbulence with cost-cutting, project cancellations, and shifts to live-service/mobile models.

- **Layoffs and Stabilization**: Significant cuts since 2022 (~30,000–45,000 jobs lost by mid-2025). 2025 saw ~9,000–14,600 layoffs (down from 2024 peaks). Hiring is recovering selectively in 2026, but with caution. Many roles have shifted to outsourcing, fixed-term contracts, or emerging markets.
- **Demand**: Over 100,000 gaming-related job postings in 2025. Strongest needs in development, engineering, design, and technical roles (e.g., programmers, AI specialists, producers). Indie, mobile, and new tech (VR/AR, AI tools) offer pockets of opportunity. AAA studios are more conservative.
- **Salaries and Conditions**: Competitive but under pressure in some areas due to supply of experienced talent from layoffs. Long-term growth projected (software dev roles ~15–22% in broader category), but entry-level is tough—high competition, with many considering leaving the industry.
- **Outlook for 2026+**: Cautious recovery expected, with growth in revenues but not necessarily headcount at legacy studios. AI is both a tool and a disruptor (helping with content but impacting some junior/asset roles). Success favors specialized skills, portfolio strength, networking, and adaptability (e.g., indie or adjacent tech).

**Entry Tips**: Build a strong portfolio, contribute to open-source/indie projects, and target in-demand skills like Unreal/Unity, multiplayer, or procedural generation. Junior roles are scarce; mid/senior or specialized experience is advantageous.

### Intersection: Quantum + Games
This niche is emerging but small. Quantum computing can enhance simulations (e.g., complex physics, procedural generation, realistic modeling in games), and games can help educate about quantum concepts. Roles might exist in R&D at tech/gaming hybrids or specialized simulators, but they are rare and usually require quantum expertise first. Not a major job driver yet.

**Overall Comparison**: Quantum simulation offers better job security, higher pay, and faster growth right now—if you can acquire the skills. Video games provide creative fulfillment and a larger ecosystem but with more volatility and competition. Both benefit from strong technical/programming foundations. The best path depends on your background, risk tolerance, and interests (e.g., physics/research vs. creative design). Markets evolve quickly, so check sites like QuantumJobs.us, GDC reports, or industry trackers for the latest.
