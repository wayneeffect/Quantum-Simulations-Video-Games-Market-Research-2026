**Entry-level jobs** in **quantum simulations** and **video games** differ significantly in accessibility, requirements, competition, and compensation as of 2026.

### Quantum Simulations / Quantum Computing
Quantum simulation roles (modeling quantum systems for chemistry, materials, physics, etc.) are in a high-demand, talent-shortage field. True "zero-experience" entry is rare due to the specialized knowledge needed, but opportunities exist through internships, junior roles, research assistant positions, and technician jobs. Many roles accept bachelor's degrees (especially in physics, computer science, math, or engineering) plus demonstrated skills.

**Common Entry-Level Roles**:
- **Junior Quantum Software Developer / Associate Quantum Developer**: Write or test quantum code, run simulations using frameworks like Qiskit or Cirq, assist with hybrid quantum-classical algorithms.
- **Quantum Research Assistant / Lab Technician / Technologist**: Help with experiments, data collection, numerical simulations of quantum systems, literature reviews, or hardware assembly/testing. Good for hands-on roles (e.g., vacuum systems, optics).
- **Quantum Intern (Applied Science, Algorithms, Hardware)**: At companies like IBM, Google, Amazon, PsiQuantum, or national labs.
- **Entry-Level Quantum Technical Analyst or Support Roles**: Basic testing, documentation, or simulation support.

**Requirements**:
- Bachelor's (or pursuing Master's) in relevant field. PhD often preferred for research but not required for many software/hardware support roles.
- Strong Python skills; familiarity with linear algebra, basic quantum mechanics.
- Hands-on experience via Qiskit, Cirq, personal projects, GitHub portfolio, hackathons, or online courses (IBM Quantum, Coursera, etc.).
- For hardware: Practical skills like electronics, prototyping.

**Pay**: Entry-level often $70,000–$100,000+ USD (higher in tech hubs), with strong growth potential.

**How to Enter**: Build projects (e.g., simulate simple molecules or spin systems), contribute to open-source quantum tools, pursue internships, and network via LinkedIn, Quantum Jobs List, or university programs. Demand outpaces supply, so motivated candidates with demonstrable skills can break in.

### Video Games
The games industry is larger and more accessible for creative/technical talent, but highly competitive with fewer junior openings due to past layoffs. Portfolios and personal projects matter far more than formal degrees. QA/testing is the classic entry point.

**Common Entry-Level Roles**:
- **QA Tester / Game Tester / Test Analyst**: Play games, report bugs, verify functionality. Often contract/on-call; gateway to other roles.
- **Junior Game Programmer / Software Engineer**: Code gameplay, UI, or tools using Unity, Unreal Engine, C#, C++.
- **Junior Artist / Animator / 3D Modeler**: Create assets, animations.
- **Production Assistant / Junior Producer**: Support project management, coordination.
- **Game Designer (Junior/Associate)**: Help with mechanics, levels (harder without portfolio).
- Other: Community support, content moderation, writing, sound roles.

**Requirements**:
- Portfolio or demo reel (e.g., completed games from jams, mods, personal projects) is essential.
- Skills in Unity/Unreal, programming, art tools (Blender, Maya), or relevant software.
- No degree always required, but computer science, game design, or art degrees help. Passion, self-taught skills, and game jams (e.g., itch.io) are key.

**Pay**: Varies widely; QA often starts lower ($40k–$60k range depending on location/role), with better compensation in programming/art. Many start with internships or contracts.

**How to Enter**: Participate in game jams, build and release small games, contribute to open-source or mods, network at events (GDC), apply via Hitmarker, GameJobs.co, company sites (e.g., Activision, Sony), and target indie studios or mobile. Internships and QA are common foot-in-the-door paths.

### Intersection (Quantum + Games)
This niche is small and emerging. It might involve using quantum techniques for advanced physics simulations, procedural generation, or optimization in games—or developing educational quantum games. Such roles are rare at entry level and usually require strong skills in one field plus interest in the other.

**Overall Advice**: 
- **Quantum** favors structured learning and technical credentials with a research bent.
- **Games** rewards creativity, shipped projects, and persistence in a volatile industry.
Check specialized job boards (Quantum Jobs List/US, Hitmarker, GameJobs.co), LinkedIn, and Indeed. Markets shift quickly—tailor applications with portfolios/projects, and consider adjacent roles (e.g., classical simulation software or general tech) as stepping stones. Both fields value demonstrated ability over pure experience.
