**Mid-level jobs** (typically 3–7 years of relevant experience) in quantum simulations and video games involve greater independence, ownership of features or projects, mentoring juniors, and deeper technical contributions compared to entry-level roles. Salaries and requirements reflect this.

### Quantum Simulations / Quantum Computing
Mid-level roles focus on designing, optimizing, and applying quantum simulation techniques for real-world problems in chemistry, materials science, physics, and optimization. Demand remains strong due to the talent shortage.

**Common Mid-Level Roles**:
- **Quantum Software Engineer** (or Quantum Simulation Engineer): Develop and optimize quantum algorithms, simulators, and hybrid quantum-classical workflows (e.g., using Qiskit, Cirq, or custom high-performance simulators).
- **Quantum Algorithm Developer / Researcher**: Design and test algorithms for simulating molecules, spin systems, or materials.
- **Quantum Systems Engineer**: Integrate hardware, software, and simulation tools; perform performance modeling and error analysis.
- **Computational Quantum Chemist / Materials Scientist**: Run and improve simulations for drug discovery, catalysis, or new materials.

**Requirements**:
- Master’s or PhD (preferred, especially for research-heavy roles) in physics, computer science, chemistry, or related fields. Strong candidates with Bachelor’s + significant experience/projects can qualify.
- Proficiency in Python/C++, quantum SDKs, linear algebra, quantum mechanics, and numerical methods. Experience with high-performance computing, error mitigation, or specific domains (e.g., quantum chemistry) is key.
- Proven contributions: Published work, open-source projects, or delivered simulation results in prior roles.

**Salaries (US, 2026)**: Typically $120,000–$200,000+ (base + bonus/equity), with mid-level quantum professionals often in the $130k–$180k range. Higher in tech hubs or at companies like IBM, Google, Microsoft, Quantinuum, or PsiQuantum.

**Growth Path**: These roles often lead to senior/lead positions or specialization in applications like quantum chemistry for industry.

### Video Games
Mid-level roles in games emphasize owning features, improving processes, and contributing to larger team efforts in a more volatile industry. Experience with shipped titles is highly valued.

**Common Mid-Level Roles**:
- **Mid-Level Game Programmer / Software Engineer**: Implement gameplay systems, physics, AI, networking, or optimization using Unity, Unreal Engine, C++, C#.
- **Mid-Level Game Designer**: Design mechanics, levels, systems, and balance; contribute to narrative or player experience.
- **Mid-Level Technical Artist / Artist**: Create pipelines, shaders, animations, or tools.
- **Mid-Level Producer / QA Lead**: Coordinate teams, manage scopes, or lead testing efforts.

**Requirements**:
- 3–7 years of experience, ideally with multiple shipped games or significant contributions.
- Strong portfolio/demo reel showing ownership of features/systems.
- Proficiency in relevant engines/tools (Unity/Unreal, Blender, etc.), programming, and domain-specific skills (e.g., multiplayer, performance optimization).
- Soft skills: Collaboration in cross-functional teams (artists, designers, engineers).

**Salaries (US, 2026)**: Mid-level game developers/programmers typically earn $90,000–$130,000+, with averages around $95k–$110k. Varies by role, location, and studio (higher at AAA or tech-adjacent companies).

**Growth Path**: Leads to senior, lead, or specialized roles (e.g., AI specialist, principal engineer). The industry rewards shipped projects and adaptability.

### Intersection (Quantum + Games)
Rare but emerging mid-level opportunities exist in advanced simulations for games (e.g., realistic physics, procedural generation via quantum-inspired methods) or educational/entertainment quantum tools. These usually require strong expertise in one domain plus interest in the other.

**Overall Advice**: 
- **Quantum** rewards advanced education, research output, and specialized technical skills — the field offers stronger security and pay growth.
- **Games** values demonstrable impact through portfolios and shipped work, with more creative variety but higher volatility.
Tailor your resume to highlight impact (e.g., “optimized simulation runtime by X%” or “shipped feature used by Y players”). Check boards like QuantumJobs.us, LinkedIn, Hitmarker, or GameJobs.co for openings, as requirements evolve. Networking and continuous learning (new tools, publications, or contributions) are essential in both fields.
